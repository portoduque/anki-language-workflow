# Pedagogy

## Objective

Optimize long-term retrieval and usable language ability while keeping reviews fast and sustainable.

## Principles

- Retrieval must be clear: the learner should know what is being tested.
- Prefer one primary retrieval target per card.
- Context should disambiguate, not give away the answer.
- Recognition and production are different skills; create both only when both matter.
- Listening is not the same as reading. Use audio-first cards when listening is the actual target.
- Chunks and collocations often deserve priority over isolated words because they support fluent production.
- Sentence mining is selective. Do not convert every sentence into a card.
- Concrete vocabulary may benefit from an image; abstract language usually benefits more from context.
- Similar new items can interfere with one another; avoid creating large batches of near-synonyms or semantic siblings without need.
- The Anki deck should make learned material retrievable. It does not replace reading, listening, speaking, or writing practice.

## Review-efficiency rule

Every additional card creates future review cost. Prefer the smallest set that covers distinct, useful retrieval skills.
